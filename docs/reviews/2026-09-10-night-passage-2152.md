## Finding 1: the on-device read still queues behind the cloud

`docs/local-recognition.md` ("Off the cloud slot") says a local grant opens the
gate while a cloud call for an older frame is in flight. The timestamps show it
cannot: `_process_bursts` is one thread, `GateProcessor.process()` runs the
local pass and then blocks in `_bounded_cloud_call` until the cloud answers, so
the next frame's local pass does not start until the previous frame's cloud
call returns. Frame 4 sat ready from 26.16 to 30.31 with a perfect plate in it.

## Finding 2: the session frames are about a second old when the controller sees them

The camera's OSD stamps put frame 3 at 09:52:23 and frame 4 at 09:52:24; the
controller decoded them at 24.14 and 25.96. Frame 1 (the FTP still) stamps
09:52:21 and landed at 21.76, so the camera clock is within a fraction of a
second of the Pi's. That bounds the steady-state stream-to-decoder lag at
**1.0-1.1 s**, on top of the 0.2-0.4 s `frame_age_ms` the controller measures
from decoder output. The deciding frame was exposed about a second before the
controller could pick it. Issue #107 quotes ~1 s of decoder start-up; this is
the lag after start-up, and it is not in any telemetry field today.

## Finding 3: the early frames cost lookups and time and can never read

At this gate the vehicle alarm fires while the car is still turning in from the
drive; the plate is oblique and small for the first ~2 s. The hot keyframe and
the FTP still are the same instant (both OSD 09:52:21), so the pipeline spent
two cloud calls (4.0 s of serial thread time) on one unreadable moment. The
`stillest` selector then handed over a frame at stillness 0.009 that was still
creeping and smeared; the frames that decide passages tonight and earlier this
evening sit at 0.001-0.002 (19:18:41, 19:51:06, 21:52:26).

## Recommendations, in order of gain

1. **Take the on-device read off the burst thread (controller change, largest
   gain).** Run the local pass for every injected frame the moment it lands,
   on a lane that never waits for a cloud response (at injection in
   `TriggerFrameCapture.capture_once`, or a dedicated consumer ahead of the
   cloud lane), and let a local grant abandon the in-flight cloud request
   (`abandon_in_flight` already exists). Tonight this opens the gate at about
   26.5 instead of 30.65: **4.7 s after the alarm instead of 8.9 s**, and about
   2.4 s after the car stopped. Across the last three days it removes a median
   1.6 s and a p90 4.1 s from every locally readable frame. Keep the
   `BoundedBurstQueue` accounting (`note_dropped`, `queue_coalesced`) and the
   presence session's one-outstanding-frame rule intact.
2. **Stop paying for frames that cannot be read.** (a) When the FTP still of
   the same alarm landed within about a second, skip the immediate hot-keyframe
   slot: it is the same instant. (b) When the local detector finds no plate and
   the frame is not still (stillness above the ~0.002 the deciding frames show)
   or predates the stop, skip the cloud fallback for that frame. `both_none` is
   53% of cloud attempts. Tonight this saves two of three lookups; after item 1
   it is quota rather than time, but the quota is 2,500 a month.
3. **Measure, then trim, the stream lag.** One camera `GetTime` call against the
   Pi clock settles the offset; then compare FTP-still time against session-frame
   OSD time over a few passages. If the ~1 s holds, the candidates are an
   always-on session (#107, removes start-up but not steady-state lag), a shorter
   encoder keyframe interval, or a 4K `Snap` through the camera-control service
   once the vehicle is still, which has no stream lag at all.
4. **Camera night ISP experiment: `nr3d` off.** At a 1/250 s shutter a creeping
   car cannot smear a digit; temporal noise reduction blending consecutive
   frames can. The night-configuration note kept `nr3d=1` on the assumption the
   vehicle is stopped at capture, and frame 3 shows the case where it is not.
   Back up, set it once, judge on the next two or three night entries from the
   corpus frames. Live camera change: owner decision.
5. **Reliability is a separate problem from speed.** Tonight the camera fired
   promptly (still 0.12 s before the webhook), ten minutes after a
   camera-control login, which weakens but does not close the hourly-login
   hypothesis for the 2026-09-09 23:25 no-alarm miss. That miss is still the
   larger night risk and still needs the camera-side check or reboot.
6. **Keep what worked.** Colour night mode with the floodlight, the
   `0,0.26,1,0.60` plate region, and the matching policy: the `131D2896` denial
   was right (6/8 is not a confusion pair, and it was one frame). Nothing in the
   exposure needs changing for the stop position.
